Collaborative Multi-Agent Systems

Developing algorithms for robot teams with different capabilities, objectives, and operating conditions. A central question: when does interaction let a team achieve something its members could not accomplish alone? The focus spans distinctions between collaboration, cooperation, and coordination, alongside resource allocation that helps teams support one another.

02
Learning from experience to guide robotic behavior.

Learning for Robotic Behavior

Exploring how learning can help robot teams decide when to share robots as transferable resources. Combining ecology-inspired models based on Hamilton’s rule with graph neural networks brings robot capabilities and transfer costs into allocation decisions. The goal is to make an NP-hard allocation problem more scalable while supporting collaborative behavior.

03
Intuitive interfaces between people and robot collectives.

Human-Swarm Interaction

Building interfaces that help people specify, supervise, and understand collective robot behavior. A particular focus is sketch-to-swarm interaction: turning freehand drawings into formation targets, robot assignments, and coordinated motion. Formation control avoids unintended reflected shapes, while a user study examines how accessible the interface is to people without robotics expertise.

Robotarium and Hardware Systems

Supporting the experimental systems that bring multi-robot algorithms into the physical world. Demonstrations and lab tours also help make the systems accessible to researchers and visitors.

Current Direction

Collaboration in Multi-Agent Systems: From Assignment to Learning

Developing ways for robot teams to share resources and combine complementary capabilities. Bringing together ecology-inspired models of altruism, optimization, and learning to guide when and how teams exchange robots. Alongside these allocation methods, exploring intuitive interfaces that let people shape collective robot behavior.
